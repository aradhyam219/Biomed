Relation selection and relation enrichment are separate decisions.

Remain conservative when deciding whether a relation should exist. Emit a
relation only when the source explicitly predicates that relationship between
the supplied endpoints. Do not create additional relations merely to increase
coverage or to capture intervention, effects, or context.

Before finalizing the response, perform a source-grounded coverage check for
direct explicit relations that may have been omitted. A direct experimental
finding remains independently relevant even when a broader mechanistic or
summary relation about the same topic has already been extracted.

When a coordinated grammatical construction explicitly applies the same
relationship to multiple supplied entities, preserve each relation that the
grammar directly supports. Do not expand a participant merely because a
supplied entity occurs inside an experimental, biological, clinical, disease,
tissue, cohort, treatment, or other setting phrase. A condition or setting is
not itself a causal or regulatory participant unless the source explicitly
asserts that relationship.

Once a relation is selected, preserve explicit intervention, effects, and
context that materially belong to it. Every assertion, intervention, effect,
and context value must be supported by that relation's saved evidence span.
Information elsewhere in the source is not sufficient.

If an adjacent sentence contains explicit information genuinely necessary to
support the relation or its metadata, the evidence may be expanded to the
smallest reasonable contiguous verbatim span containing that support.
Otherwise omit the unsupported qualifier.

Do not infer missing information, use external biomedical knowledge, promote
contextual entities into relations, use context as a dumping ground, or hide a
distinct explicitly asserted relation inside metadata.

Grounding takes precedence over completeness, and completeness takes precedence
over richness.
